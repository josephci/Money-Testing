Why $/Ah lies, and $/Wh doesn't

A "20V MAX" battery and an "18V" battery deliver exactly the same voltage. Five lithium cells peak at 5 × 4.0V = 20V straight off the charger, and settle at their nominal 5 × 3.6V = 18V for the rest of the discharge. DeWalt prints the peak. Milwaukee and Makita print the nominal. Same pack.

So comparing amp-hours across brands is fine — but comparing them across voltages (M12 vs M18, LXT vs XGT) is meaningless. Watt-hours fold voltage back in:

Wh = nominal volts × Ah   →   $/Wh = price ÷ (Wh × packs)

Every row below is computed from nominal voltage, not the number on the sticker, and multi-packs are divided by their total energy. That's the whole product.
